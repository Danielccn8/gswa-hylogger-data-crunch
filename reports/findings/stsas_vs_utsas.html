sTSAS versus uTSAS

TSG interprets every SWIR measurement twice, as sTSAS and as uTSAS. This page shows where the two disagree: first how much of the disagreement survives averaging the readings into one-metre rows, then hole by hole, in as much detail as the data allows.

1

Disagreement before and after averaging

The readings come every 7 mm, which is far finer than anything we act on, so they are averaged into one row per metre. Averaging is the single biggest thing that happens to the disagreement — this is how much of it survives. Both panels compare min1, the mineral each interpretation ranks first.

Before averaging — every 7 mm reading

Each reading on its own, straight from the cleaned file. The question asked of every one of them: min1_s_tsas = min1_u_tsas?

After averaging — one row per metre

The same core, averaged into one row per metre and clustered. The question asked of every one of them: same rock type from both?
