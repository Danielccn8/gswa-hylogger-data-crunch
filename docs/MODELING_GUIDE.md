# Modeling guide

One model row is a depth interval for one sensor family. SWIR (`uTSAS`) and TIR (`ujCLST`) remain separate. The feature vector is TSG group frequency among admitted rows, transformed by a square root so sparse compositions compare with Hellinger-like geometry. K-Means fits centroids in that transformed space. PCA is **not** used by this first runner; a PCA view would represent variance but would not itself create clusters.

The runner reserves the last sorted hole as a test hole when at least three holes are available. It chooses `k` from 2, 3 and 4 using silhouette on development rows only, using at most 2,000 rows per score. This is a limited internal separation measure and can be dominated by repeated near-neighbour readings. It is not independent geological validation. With only two holes, the card records no test hole. The model card lists features, candidate scores, cluster sizes and original-frequency profiles. It should be inspected before interpreting a cluster.

Inference loads `model.joblib` and applies the frozen feature order and transform. A new group name or another spectral region is rejected for review. Retraining generates a separate model ID. The app selects an explicit active model and preserves prior assignments.

The dominant TSG group is the simplest baseline and remains visible beside every model group. The current cards compare direct frequencies and PCA representations on the development population, alongside the selected square-root representation. On 26 holes, SWIR selected k=4 and had 0.370 silhouette on the held-out YRC10-13 hole; TIR selected k=3 and had 0.519. These are internal separation scores, not accuracy against geology. Three-seed development ARI values were 0.992 and 0.999. Original-frequency cluster profiles and sizes are in each card and in the application's **How the groups were made** panel.

The bounded HDBSCAN comparator samples at most 80 development intervals per hole and reports labelled coverage, noise and silhouette on non-noise rows. At min cluster size 20, SWIR leaves 71.41% as noise; TIR leaves 79.84%. The high non-noise silhouette is therefore selective. It was not deployed as a replacement. Sensitivity at 0.5/1/2 m was checked on two holes; scanner, tray and `s/u` processing effects remain unmeasured.

Model IDs include scikit-learn, NumPy and pandas versions; inference checks these before unpickling a model card with recorded dependencies. Frozen inference on 05GJD001 assigned 166 SWIR and 166 TIR intervals without changing active models. That hole was already in development, so this verifies the mechanism, not generalisation. TSG interpretations and their spectra are related, so agreement is not independent truth. Assay/lithology matching and GSWA review remain external tasks.

The bounded spectral script reads **adjusted spectra from extracted TSG packages**, not uncorrected full-resolution instrument raw data. It checks Final Mask and wavelength compatibility, fits SNV/scaling/PCA/K-Means on one hole, and assigns a second with a training-only distance threshold. Its model card and mean spectra live in `data/system/spectral/<region>`. Two holes are an engineering contrast, not a generalisation estimate.

The 05GJD001→LD82625W1 SWIR pilot flags 585/642 inference intervals outside its development distance threshold, while TIR flags 32/642. The interface draws the selected interval's adjusted spectrum and the development-cluster mean, and labels the distance status. The substantial SWIR shift is evidence against treating this pilot as a statewide model.
